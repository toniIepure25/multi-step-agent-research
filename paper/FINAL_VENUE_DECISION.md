# Final Venue Decision

## Venue Comparison

| Criterion | ICLR 2027 | ICML 2027 | NeurIPS D&B 2027 | ACL 2027 |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| Fit | Good | Good | Very Good | Good |
| Page limit | 9 pages | 8 pages | 8 pages | 8 pages |
| Reviewer pool | ML generalists | ML generalists | Evaluation specialists | NLP specialists |
| Preregistration value | Medium | Medium | High | Medium |
| Negative result tolerance | Medium | Medium | High | Medium |
| Methodology focus | Valued | Valued | Core | Valued |
| RAG/agent relevance | High | High | Medium | Very High |
| Estimated acceptance rate | ~25% | ~25% | ~30% | ~25% |

## Analysis

### ICLR 2027
**Fit: BORDERLINE SUBMIT**
- Pros: Large ML audience, high visibility, values empirical rigor
- Cons: May view as "just evaluation," hostile reviewers may dismiss
- Risk: R5-type reviewer kills it

### ICML 2027
**Fit: BORDERLINE SUBMIT**
- Similar to ICLR. Slightly more methodological focus.

### NeurIPS 2027 Datasets & Benchmarks Track
**Fit: STRONG SUBMIT**
- Pros: Values evaluation methodology, tolerates negative results, appreciates reproducibility
- Cons: Lower visibility than main tracks, may not reach agent/RAG audience
- This is the SAFEST option

### ACL 2027
**Fit: BORDERLINE SUBMIT**
- Pros: NLP audience directly cares about RAG, retrieval, and evidence integration
- Cons: May want more NLP-specific analysis (linguistic features, error categories)

## Decision

**PRIMARY: ICLR 2027** — Submit to the main track. The paper's methodology and finding are strong enough to compete. The risk of a hostile reviewer exists but is manageable with clear novelty positioning.

**FALLBACK: NeurIPS 2027 Datasets & Benchmarks** — If ICLR rejects, this is a near-certain accept given the methodology, reproducibility, and trace dataset.

**Classification: ICLR BORDERLINE SUBMIT**

The paper is not a guaranteed accept but has a realistic chance (~40-50%) given the strong methodology, clear empirical finding, and honest negative results. The key risk is a hostile reviewer who considers the finding obvious.
