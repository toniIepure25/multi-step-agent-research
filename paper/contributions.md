# Contributions

1. **A controlled benchmark for counterfactual evaluation of cognitive operation sequences.** We introduce an epistemic world simulator with latent ground truth that enables same-state counterfactual comparison of alternative cognitive operations and sequences, isolating the downstream value of temporal composition from confounding state variation.

2. **Empirical evidence of temporal complementarity and interference among cognitive operations.** In controlled experiments across eight heterogeneous epistemic regimes, we measure super-additive gains (hypothesis generation → retrieval: +0.162), interference (repeated hypothesis generation: −0.050), and strong timing dependence (attack value: 0.000 early vs. +0.276 late).

3. **LLM-substrate replication of temporal complementarity and timing effects.** We replicate the two strongest controlled effects — temporal complementarity (+0.111) and attack timing (+0.276) — under real inference with two materially different language models (Gemma 3 27B QAT, Llama 3.2 11B), confirming these as structural properties of the epistemic task.

4. **A systematic negative-results analysis bounding the scope of cognitive sequencing.** We report that general sequence superiority, operation ordering effects, adaptive motif control, and real-document transfer are not supported by our experiments, establishing that the value of cognitive sequencing is operation-pair-specific rather than universal.
