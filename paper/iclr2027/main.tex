\begin{abstract}
Intermediate reasoning artifacts---hypotheses, analysis plans, reformulated queries---are increasingly used to guide evidence retrieval in multi-step LLM systems. Improved retrieval is commonly treated as evidence that such cognitive interventions are beneficial. We challenge this assumption through a preregistered causal audit across SciFact (claim verification) and HotpotQA (multi-hop QA), using Gemma~27B and Llama~11B under five matched-resource conditions that isolate artifact semantics from generic query quality.

We find a robust \emph{retrieval--reasoning dissociation}: real hypothesis artifacts significantly improve gold evidence recall over shuffled controls across all four dataset--model cells ($+0.18$ to $+0.51$, all $p < .002$ after Holm correction over 14 tests). However, this retrieval gain improves downstream F1 on HotpotQA ($+0.12$, $p < .002$) while degrading accuracy on SciFact ($-0.23$ to $-0.39$, $p < .002$). Generic query expansion---without any hypothesis content---matches or exceeds hypothesis-conditioned retrieval, indicating the benefit is not hypothesis-specific. Answer flip analysis and artifact visibility ablation localize the SciFact failure to evidence integration rather than hypothesis anchoring: more retrieved evidence introduces more reasoning errors. These findings demonstrate that retrieval improvement is not a reliable proxy for end-to-end agent utility.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multi-step LLM systems increasingly employ intermediate reasoning artifacts---hypotheses, sub-question decompositions, analysis plans---to guide evidence retrieval \citep{hyde2023,query2doc2023,selfrag2024}. When such artifacts improve retrieval quality (e.g., gold evidence recall), the intervention is typically considered beneficial. But does improved retrieval reliably translate to improved task performance?

We show that it does not. Using a preregistered causal audit with five matched-resource conditions across two benchmarks and two model families, we find a \textbf{retrieval--reasoning dissociation}: the same type of intermediate artifact that reliably improves retrieval ($+0.18$ to $+0.51$ gold recall, all four dataset--model cells significant after Holm correction) produces \emph{opposite} downstream effects depending on task structure. On HotpotQA (multi-hop QA), improved retrieval leads to improved F1 ($+0.12$, $p < .002$). On SciFact (scientific claim verification), improved retrieval leads to \emph{degraded} accuracy ($-0.23$ to $-0.39$, $p < .002$).

This dissociation has practical implications for agent design. Retrieval quality metrics---commonly used to evaluate and optimize RAG systems---can be directionally misleading about system utility. A component that succeeds at its local objective (finding relevant evidence) may fail at the global objective (producing correct answers) when the bottleneck shifts from evidence acquisition to evidence integration.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A resource-matched, trace-certified intervention framework for auditing intermediate artifacts at each pipeline stage (\S\ref{sec:method}).
    \item Empirical evidence of a retrieval--reasoning dissociation across two datasets and two models (\S\ref{sec:dissociation}).
    \item A demonstration that generic query expansion achieves comparable retrieval to task-specific hypotheses (\S\ref{sec:generic}).
    \item A mechanistic diagnosis localizing the SciFact failure to evidence integration rather than hypothesis anchoring (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What did NOT survive validation.}
Initial experiments suggested temporal complementarity between cognitive operations and hypothesis-specific retrieval advantages. Compute-matched factorial controls eliminated the first, and generic expansion baselines eliminated the second. These falsifications motivated the final causal audit design reported here.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hypothesis-conditioned retrieval.}
HyDE \citep{hyde2023} generates hypothetical documents to improve dense retrieval; Query2Doc \citep{query2doc2023} uses LLM-generated pseudo-documents as query expansions. Both evaluate retrieval quality but do not trace downstream reasoning effects of the retrieval improvement.

\paragraph{Adaptive RAG and retrieval utility.}
FLARE \citep{flare2023} and Self-RAG \citep{selfrag2024} adaptively decide when and how to retrieve. IRCoT \citep{ircot2023} interleaves retrieval with chain-of-thought reasoning. These works assume retrieval is beneficial and optimize its timing; we question whether retrieval improvement is reliably beneficial.

\paragraph{Context distraction and conflict.}
Recent work shows that irrelevant or contradictory retrieved context can harm generation \citep{notrust2024,lostmiddle2024}. Our finding is distinct: we show that \emph{improved} retrieval of \emph{relevant} evidence can harm task performance---not because the evidence is bad, but because the model cannot reliably integrate it.

\paragraph{Agent evaluation.}
AgentBench \citep{agentbench2024} and GAIA \citep{gaia2024} evaluate end-to-end agent performance. We instead audit one specific pipeline component (intermediate artifact) and decompose its effects at each stage.

\section{Causal Artifact Auditing Framework}
\label{sec:method}

We define a pipeline with five stages: (1)~artifact generation, (2)~query formation, (3)~evidence retrieval, (4)~evidence integration, and (5)~final decision.

\paragraph{Conditions.}
We compare five conditions that vary the artifact while holding compute, retrieval budget, and model constant:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textsc{Real}: Task-specific hypothesis generated by the model.
    \item \textsc{Shuffled}: Hypothesis from a different randomly-selected task (same format, irrelevant content).
    \item \textsc{Neutral}: Generic placeholder artifact (``No specific analysis available'').
    \item \textsc{Generic}: LLM-generated query expansion without hypothesis commitment.
    \item \textsc{Direct}: Raw task question used directly as retrieval query.
\end{itemize}

Two diagnostic conditions are used on SciFact only:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textsc{Parametric-Only}: Model answers without any retrieval.
    \item \textsc{Real-Hidden}: Same retrieval as \textsc{Real}, but hypothesis hidden from the answer model.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Resource matching.}
All retrieval-based conditions use the same model, the same BM25 retriever with $k=5$, and the same number of LLM calls (3 per task). Table~\ref{tab:setup} details the resource budget.

\paragraph{Datasets.}
SciFact \citep{scifact2020}: 188 evaluable claims requiring scientific evidence assessment. HotpotQA \citep{hotpotqa2018}: multi-hop questions requiring evidence from multiple Wikipedia articles.

\paragraph{Models.}
Gemma~27B (gemma3:27b-it-qat) as the primary model on unseen tasks, and Llama~11B (llama3.2-vision:11b-instruct-q8\_0) as the transfer model on seen tasks.

\paragraph{Statistical analysis.}
Task-level paired bootstrap (10{,}000 resamples) with Holm correction over 14 simultaneous tests. Preregistered at SHA \texttt{8950ba9} before locked execution.

\section{Intermediate Artifacts Improve Retrieval}
\label{sec:retrieval}

\textsc{Real} hypothesis artifacts significantly improve gold evidence retrieval over \textsc{Shuffled} controls in all four dataset--model cells (Table~\ref{tab:effects}). Effect sizes range from $+0.18$ (HotpotQA$\times$Gemma) to $+0.51$ (SciFact$\times$Llama), all $p_\text{Holm} < .002$. Semantic content in the intermediate artifact causally directs retrieval toward relevant evidence.

\subsection{But Hypotheses Are Not Special}
\label{sec:generic}

\textsc{Generic} query expansion---which reformulates the question without committing to any hypothesis---achieves comparable or superior retrieval. In 2 of 4 cells, \textsc{Generic} significantly \emph{outperforms} \textsc{Real} after Holm correction (SciFact$\times$Gemma: $-0.18$, $p = .007$; HotpotQA$\times$Gemma: $-0.07$, $p = .024$). In no cell does \textsc{Real} significantly outperform \textsc{Generic}.

This means the retrieval benefit is not attributable uniquely to hypothesis semantics. Any retrieval-oriented intermediate reformulation can produce similar gains.

\section{The Retrieval--Reasoning Dissociation}
\label{sec:dissociation}

Despite uniformly positive retrieval effects, downstream task performance splits by dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:plane}).

On \textbf{HotpotQA}, improved retrieval translates to improved F1: \textsc{Real} vs \textsc{Shuffled} yields $+0.12$ ($p_\text{Holm} < .002$, Gemma). More retrieved evidence provides more facts for multi-hop answer extraction.

On \textbf{SciFact}, improved retrieval \emph{degrades} accuracy: \textsc{Real} vs \textsc{Shuffled} yields $-0.23$ ($p_\text{Holm} < .002$, Gemma) and $-0.39$ ($p_\text{Holm} < .002$, Llama). More retrieved scientific evidence introduces more reasoning errors.

This pattern replicates qualitatively across both model families (Figure~\ref{fig:forest}).

\section{Why Does SciFact Fail?}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\paragraph{Not hypothesis anchoring.}
When the hypothesis artifact is hidden from the answer model (\textsc{Real-Hidden}), accuracy improves by only 4.5pp (Gemma) and 6.0pp (Llama)---neither significant after Holm correction ($p = .40$ and $p = .33$). The artifact's direct influence on reasoning is small.

\paragraph{Evidence integration failure.}
The primary mechanism is the model's inability to correctly integrate retrieved scientific evidence. Table~\ref{tab:flips} shows the answer flip decomposition against a \textsc{Parametric-Only} baseline:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textsc{Shuffled} retrieves zero gold evidence (recall = 0.000) and achieves near-perfect accuracy (1.00 Gemma, 0.96 Llama) with zero harmful flips (CW = 0).
    \item \textsc{Real} retrieves substantial gold evidence (recall = 0.28--0.51) but produces harmful flips (CW = 2--8) and wrong-stays-wrong cases (WW = 18--35).
    \item \textsc{Generic} retrieves the most evidence (recall = 0.46--0.58) and produces the most wrong-stays-wrong cases (WW = 24--40).
\end{itemize}

More retrieved evidence introduces more opportunities for reasoning errors. The model defaults to high-accuracy patterns when no evidence is present, but introduces errors when asked to assess complex scientific evidence.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Retrieval metrics as misleading proxies.}
Our findings demonstrate that retrieval quality metrics (gold evidence recall) can be directionally misleading about system utility. An agent component that scores well on retrieval evaluation may actively harm the system when the downstream bottleneck is evidence integration rather than evidence acquisition.

\paragraph{Implications for agent design.}
If metacognitive policies optimize intermediate proxies---retrieval relevance, evidence recall, artifact quality---they may choose actions that improve the proxy while worsening final task utility. This pattern is structurally analogous to reward misspecification: a local proxy is optimized at the expense of a global objective.

\paragraph{When does retrieval help?}
Our results suggest retrieval helps when the task requires \emph{extracting facts} from retrieved passages (HotpotQA) but can hurt when the task requires \emph{assessing complex evidence} (SciFact). This distinction may generalize: evidence-extraction tasks benefit from more evidence, while evidence-assessment tasks suffer from it.

\paragraph{Generic reformulation suffices.}
The negative result on hypothesis specificity (PV-H2) has practical implications. For retrieval improvement alone, elaborate hypothesis formation is unnecessary; simple query expansion achieves comparable results. Hypothesis formation may serve other purposes (reasoning scaffolding, explainability) but not retrieval optimization.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Two datasets. The task-dependent claim rests on one positive and one negative case. Additional datasets would strengthen the pattern.
    \item Two model families (Gemma 27B, Llama 11B). We do not claim model invariance.
    \item BM25 retrieval only. Results may differ with dense retrievers.
    \item Explicit externalized artifacts only. Internal chain-of-thought reasoning may behave differently.
    \item No human subjects. The mechanistic diagnosis is computational, not validated with human evidence assessment.
    \item The causal pipeline decomposition is observational between stages; formal causal mediation analysis was not conducted.
\end{itemize}

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}

We presented a preregistered causal audit of intermediate cognitive artifacts in LLM retrieval pipelines, revealing a robust retrieval--reasoning dissociation: the same type of artifact that improves evidence retrieval produces opposite downstream effects depending on task structure. This finding, replicated across two model families, demonstrates that retrieval quality is not a reliable proxy for end-to-end agent utility. The bottleneck in retrieval-augmented systems may be evidence integration rather than evidence acquisition---a distinction with implications for how we evaluate, optimize, and trust multi-step LLM agents.
